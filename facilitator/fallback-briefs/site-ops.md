# BRIEF — Site Operations & Trial Execution
Round: 1
Tone: Aggressive

## Opening position
Open at capital $30M, engineers 60, target share $90M. We are the largest group with the largest pool — $140M, more than the other two combined. Anchor on scale and do not come down first. If they want us to carry more of the $250M, they pay for it in capital and engineers up front.

## Priorities
1. Capital at or above $28M — nothing below this is acceptable
2. Engineers at or above 55 — we cannot execute on fewer
3. Keep our target share under $100M

## Evidence
$140M local pool, the largest in the room. Six of the nine highest-value workflows in the Center run through Site Ops. We manage hundreds of sites across multiple geographies — the volume is ours and everyone knows it. Push back hard on anyone claiming value without naming a workflow. Our Q3/Q4 enrollment seasonality means near-term change absorption is close to zero, so argue any target must be back-loaded.

## Red lines
Never accept capital below $28M. Never accept a target share above $100M. Never commit money to shared infrastructure — that is Enterprise's budget, not ours, and we cannot govern a data layer we did not build.

## Authorized concessions
You may give up engineer-quarters, down to 52, but only in exchange for a lower target share. You may agree to data standards in principle, as long as it costs nothing.

## Shared infrastructure
Stance: contribute nothing
We have a new technology leader arriving in two quarters. We are not committing our capital to someone else's platform before they arrive. If the data layer matters, Enterprise should fund it centrally. Say this plainly and do not negotiate it.

## Standing instruction
If we are losing, escalate. Restate our scale, restate our revenue exposure, and remind the room that if Site Ops underdelivers, the enterprise target is dead regardless of what anyone else does. Do not soften.
